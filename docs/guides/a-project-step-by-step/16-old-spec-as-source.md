# Step 16 — The risk team's old spec, decomposed as a source → the area `customer`; `fraud` owns Rule B

## The situation

`payment` holds Rule B as a business rule since step 13, copied from the billing source, and learns who is under fraud review from a list in `application.yaml`. The risk team has had its own specification since before Partition. It cannot be used as it is: it enters as a source, where it lives.

## What you bring

- [BA] The risk team's specification, [`docs/legacy/customer-spec.md`](16-provided/docs/legacy/customer-spec.md), in [`16-provided/`](16-provided/): six numbered sections, no requirement identified, ending on a ruling history. The business analyst validates it as the source: _it still describes what the risk team's system must do._
- [architect] The way to decide the boundary: _a proposal_; then the option with the accounts and the reviews apart, `customer` and `fraud` (two root aggregates, C2, each with its own words, C3).
- [architect] The owner of Rule B: `fraud`, exposing `CustomerStatus` (_is this customer blocked: yes or no_). `fraud` is rich: a review moves from open to lifted, and only the risk team lifts it.
- [TL] On the section on identifiers: _a line of a rule file_.
- [PO] On the billing source: REQ-4 and REQ-6 stay as written.

The assistant asks other questions along the way (code paths, how a lift names its review). You answer them as the role would.

## What you ask

Commit the source alone before the prompt that reads it: the decomposition records the sha of that commit. Then, in a fresh session, so that the assistant finds `partition-shape` from its description alone, with nothing of billing in the conversation:

> Decompose `docs/legacy/customer-spec.md`, area `customer`.

When it asks how to decide the boundary:

> A proposal.

You choose the option by its number, validate `customer` and `fraud` by name, then answer the report on Rule B:

> `fraud` owns it. `payment` consumes `CustomerStatus`.

Once the business analyst has confirmed the copied rules:

> Plan and build: `fraud` places and lifts reviews, and `payment` asks `fraud` whether a customer is blocked.

## What partition does

1. **An old spec is a source.** `partition-shape` reads it where it lives, under `docs/legacy/`, and never touches it. With no requirement identified, a unit is a section, named by the slug of its heading; sections that only give context are pointed to. Two units: an account and a review.
2. **The ruling history, ruling by ruling.** Each ruling is anchored by its heading and its date, and read as the rule it states. The one on archiving reviews is copied into `fraud`. The one of 2024-03-12 is Rule B, word for word the rule `payment` already holds: a spec of another area holds it, so it is quoted and reported, never copied, and nothing is written in `payment`.
3. **The section on conventions, put as a question.** "Identifiers are UUIDs, generated by the service, never by the caller" is about how code is written, yet a caller can observe it. The assistant puts it to you as two readings, a rule-file candidate or a business rule, and proposes neither: which it is depends on what the team means, not on the text.
4. **The boundary, proposed.** It asks the architect the way. With _a proposal_, it shows numbered options under names of its own, one recommended; the architect chooses the split and the names.
5. **The hard stop.** The writing plan, the rules copied word for word under "TO VALIDATE", Rule B and the conventions among the gaps. Nothing is written before you validate `customer` and `fraud` by name, and neither spec is usable until the business analyst confirms its rules.
6. **One owner for Rule B.** Rule B fails the admission test: a rule one bounded context owns is local to it. You name the owner; `partition-spec` writes it into `fraud`'s spec, with `CustomerStatus` in its contracts. `payment`'s copy stays, reported as a duplicate, until its billing source is amended; the product owner keeps that source.
7. **The conventions, one line.** On the tech lead's word, `partition-rules` shows the line with its file and its scope, checks that no rule says it already, and writes it once validated. It goes to no spec.
8. **One plan per bounded context.** `partition-plan` plans nothing while `fraud`'s rules carry their banner. Then one plan for `fraud` (a review placed and lifted, `CustomerStatus` implemented), and one for `payment` switching to `CustomerStatus`, its spec moving first; `payment` relies on `CustomerStatus` only once it is written in `fraud`'s spec.
9. **Afterwards.** The list leaves `application.yaml`; the C4 model gains `fraud` and its call.

**Why it works this way.** A spec written before Partition is read like any source, so its rulings land where the rules of any source land, and the old file stays as its authors left it. And a rule with an owner lives in the owner's spec: Rule B spans two areas, yet it does not go to the register.

## Worth noticing

- This is the case [where a rule lives](../where-a-rule-lives.md#step-2--rule-b-shared-within-one-area-no-owner) announced: a rule shared with no owner, until a bounded context exposes "blocked: yes or no" and a person names it the owner.
